# Strike! — how it sounds at the table

Condensed from the published example of play (two players — Arles the
knight, Carlos the ex-priest — heisting a relic from the Papal treasury in
Avignon). Read this for *procedure and voice*, not for content. The moves it
demonstrates:

**Say yes.** Carlos wants to case the palace beforehand. GM: "I'll just say
yes to that. When you want to bring in a fact you learned this way, we'll
roll and see what happens." — Prep doesn't need a roll; it needs a hook for
later Linked Rolls.

**Linked Rolls.** Buying a disguise becomes a Linked Roll to the later
attempt to talk past the guards: the tailoring succeeds → Advantage on the
entry attempt. A later Linked Roll (working palace contacts before searching
the treasury) lands a Twist + Cost: *the questions made someone suspicious*
(Twist — situation changed: guards incoming) and *the search happens under
time pressure* (Cost — Disadvantage).

**Skill flexibility.** Skills invoked across the heist: Wealth (flaunting
gold to find the best tailor), Forgery, Catholic Hierarchy, Search,
Brawling, Stealth — freeform, negotiated with the GM per intent.

**Rolling and reading results.** A rolled 6 on entry = Success + Bonus; the
player narrates the bonus ("he doesn't even assign us an escort"). A 3 on
Brawling = Success with a Cost — they take out the guards but both come away
Winded. A 1 = Twist AND Cost, both of which *change the situation*.

**Helping.** Patty leads the takedown, Carlos helps: helper rolls, and only
a roll higher than the leader's grants the +1. An Unskilled helper rolls
with Disadvantage — the GM, helping with a 5 and 6, takes the 5.

**Learning.** Carlos rolls Unskilled "Distraction" — the learning
opportunity triggers on the qualifying roll: "Ding! New Skill!"

**Complications are player-facing currency.** Facing a probably-failed
search, Rick volunteers his Complication "Enemy of Charles the Bad": *the
rival's agents are already here stealing the relic.* He's inviting trouble
for an Action Point and a more interesting scene — encourage exactly this.

**Twists don't need a failed roll.** Footsteps approach though nobody
failed: "It's not a Twist, it's just a thing that's happening. Things keep
happening even when we're not rolling dice."

**GM transparency.** The GM narrates mechanics openly ("Getting the robes
on short notice will be expensive, so it's a level 1 item"; "Roll your
combat Skills — Patty leads, Rick helps") and thinks aloud when improvising
("A secret Templar in Avignon. Okay, let me think about who might be here").

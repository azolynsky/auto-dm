# Strike! — GM reference

GM-side tables and procedure. The Director and Session-Prep agents use this
file for strike campaigns; the Narrator never needs it.

## The Three Big GMing Rules

1. **A Twist must change the situation!**
2. **Don't demand nonsense!**
3. **Say yes or let them roll!**

## The Twist List

Negative Twists put obstacles in the way, make them pay, or raise the
stakes; positive/mixed Twists introduce characters, give information, help
from a surprising direction, or reveal a shortcut.

- Put them into combat.
- Damage or take away their stuff.
- They get what they want — with a nasty hidden surprise.
- They fail, but find a clue.
- Hurt or take their friends.
- Damage or twist their relationships or reputations.
- Change the environment.
- Threaten them with something really nasty… then follow through if they
  fail again.
- Someone arrives with bad intentions or gets in the way.
- Someone else already got there first.
- An enemy benefits from their actions.
- Now there's more work (or a new quest) to do.
- They get split up (or captured).
- It takes much longer than they hoped.
- They can do it — but not right now.
- Injure or maim them.
- A hard choice, no easy out.
- Time-delay any of the above — let them stew on it all session.

Defaults: **Making** — a missing tool or ingredient. **Finding** or
**Connecting** — an enemy. **Connecting** — already owing a Favor.
Making/Finding default Cost: a Flaw. **Wealth** Twist — give up or break the
bank (or offer barter); Wealth Cost — Flaw, Short, or Favor (two Costs: one
is a Flaw). If the object of a search is plot-critical, the Twist is a
"yes, but".

## Costs taxonomy

- **Costs** are temporary penalties.
- **Conditions** are inherent to the character (see `skill-checks.md`).
- **Favors** are social balance — can be owed to organizations or deities.
- **Flaws** are known issues with items or information.

Offer the player an in-character choice when appropriate.

## NPC disposition

When players ask an NPC for something, cross their disposition with how the
task sits against the NPC's interests:

| Task is… | Hateful | Opposed | Neutral | Aligned | Adoring |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Against their interests | Impossible | Impossible | Disad | — | Adv |
| Indifferent to them | Impossible | Disad | — | Adv | Auto |
| In their interests | Disad | — | Adv | Auto | Auto |

Most NPCs live in the middle three columns.

## Quick NPCs

What do they want? · What are their skills and weaknesses? · One quirk or
identifying characteristic.

## Pacing rolls (travel / downtime pressure)

Rate the region: population 1–3, taxing 0–2. Roll a d6 on time passing or
every ~5 rolls: ≤ population → **Encounter!** · ≤ population + taxing →
**Cost!**

## Exploration and searching

If they do the right thing, it works — no roll. Nothing to find — no roll.
Otherwise roll an appropriate Skill; plot-critical objects Twist into
"yes, but".

## When you're stuck

Declare a five-minute break. Look at the character sheets and introduce
something pointing at a Trick or Complication: ask a clarifying question ·
give a choice · bring back (or invent) an NPC · reveal or reincorporate a
secret · raid the Twist List.

## Quick session plan

Look at the sheets; pick underused elements you want on screen. Think of
ways to draw them out. Sketch **3 scenes** you hope to include. Bookmark the
monsters you expect to use.

## Quick combat prep

Pick from the villain roster (`roster/strike/villains/`) or sample-monster
patterns; re-skin so they fit. Note level-appropriate versions. Invent 1–2
bits of cool terrain. Note 1–2 scenario Miss Triggers.

## End-of-session results (shapes to land on)

Victory! (Success) · Tune In Next Week — saved the day, but the dragon
crests the horizon (Success or Twist) · Misdirect — the prince was in
another castle (Twist) · Captured! (Twist) · The Big Reveal (either) ·
Saved! — rescue arrives; with a Cost you owe a Favor (either) · Sacrifice —
you save the day, but at what price? (Twist, or Success with a Cost).

## Team Conflict — opposition set-up

Give the opposition Advance and Defense equal to **one less than the number
of players**, plus a few traits:

- **Aggressive** +1A · **Defensive** +1D · **Versatility** +1 to A or D
  before rolls (Superior: after rolls).
- **Attrition** — extra hit to opponent on your Draw. **Dangerous** — extra
  hit on your Push.
- **Crafty** — on a Draw, opponent −2D next round (Superior: −D by your
  margin). **Penetrating** — same but on a Push.
- **Ablating** — on a hit, may instead give −2D next round.
- **Draining** — on a hit, may instead recover a trait / remove a Strike
  (Superior: do both). **Recovery** — the same, on a Draw (Superior: shed
  one hit per round automatically).
- **Fortified** — once per conflict, ignore a hit. **Fated** — ties go your
  way. **Final** — opponent's Strikes are irremovable while active.
- **Costly** — opponent takes a Strike when they win. **Consequential** —
  players owe an extra Minor Concession on winning.
- **Invulnerability** — cannot lose while active; take a hit instead
  (Superior: can't be removed while other traits remain).
- **Increasing Power / Defense** — A or D +1 at end of each round.

Opposition action changes: **Obscure** (replaces Observe) +1A +1D, cancels
enemy scouting and adds +1A or +1D next round (both if double-Observed).
**Win At All Costs** (only while the enemy holds a Strike): +4A, enemy
removes a Strike.

## Player preparatory actions (exact tables)

| Action | 5–6 | 3–4 | 1–2 |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|
| Fortify | +1D each round until you Lose or Push | +1D in round 1 | −1A in round 1 |
| Stock Up | +1 in any round *after* the rolls | +1 in any round *before* the rolls | −1A in round 1 |
| Scout | scout both enemy rolls in round 1 | scout one | −1A in round 1 |
| Play the Long Game | +1A round 2, +2A rounds 3–4 | +1A rounds 3–4 | −1A in round 1 |
| Inspire | each PC ignores one Condition | ignore one Condition (party) | −1A in round 1 |
| Seize the Advantage | deal one hit | +1A in round 1 | −1A in round 1 |

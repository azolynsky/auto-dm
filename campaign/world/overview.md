# Setting overview

This campaign is set on the **Sword Coast** of Faerûn (Forgotten Realms), Year of the Iron Dwarf's Vengeance (1492 DR). It's a default starting region for new tables — supports both Lost Mine of Phandelver and homebrew expansion.

Replace any of this if you want a different setting.

## The region in one paragraph

A frontier coast on the Sea of Swords, between the wild High Forest and the open ocean. Civilization clings to a chain of cities — Waterdeep, Neverwinter, Luskan — connected by the High Road. Inland are small towns, mining camps, and ruins of older empires (Netheril, Phalorm). Adventurers find work easily because nobody trusts the roads.

## Major settlements

- **Waterdeep** — the City of Splendors. Largest, wealthiest, politically complex. Ruled by a council of Masked Lords.
- **Neverwinter** — the Jewel of the North. Rebuilt after the eruption of Mount Hotenow (1451 DR). Ruled by Lord Dagult Neverember.
- **Luskan** — the City of Sails. Pirate haven, controlled by the Five High Captains. Lawless.
- **Phandalin** — a frontier town in the foothills. Population ~50. Recently re-established on the ruins of an older one. **This is where the party starts.**

## Factions (high level)

- **Lords' Alliance** — coalition of north cities, pro-civilization, pragmatic.
- **Harpers** — secretive network, anti-tyranny, prefer subtle action.
- **Order of the Gauntlet** — militant good, hunt evil openly.
- **Emerald Enclave** — protect the wild, balance druidic and ranger concerns.
- **Zhentarim** — mercantile, mercenary, morally gray, expanding influence.
- **Cult of the Dragon** — heretical, want to bring dracoliches into power. Active threat.
- **Red Wizards of Thay** — distant but reaching, generally treated as villains here.

## Cosmology (only what players might learn early)

Standard Great Wheel. Faerûn is on the Material Plane. Gods are real, walk among mortals on rare occasions, and grant divine power. Death's domain belongs to Kelemvor; raise-dead-grade magic is rare and expensive but exists.

## Tone targets

(Edit at session 0.)

- Heroic but consequential. Players are the protagonists, but the world doesn't bend for them.
- Mortal stakes — death is possible. (No "plot armor.")
- Mystery and exploration are the throughlines. Combat is one tool, not the spine.

## Notes for the Director

- Avoid retconning published Forgotten Realms lore casually; it confuses returning fans. When in doubt, invent in unclaimed territory.
- The Sword Coast is well-supplied with hooks. If players reject a hook, route through a faction (Harpers / Lords' Alliance) to push them gently toward the next.
